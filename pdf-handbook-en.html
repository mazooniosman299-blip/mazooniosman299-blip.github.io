Practical PDF handbook: merge, compress and convert documents safely

A practical guide to PDF merging, compression trade-offs, JPG versus PNG, safe handling and verification before sharing documents.

Getting a PDF into the right format is only half the job. A document must also open correctly for its recipient, preserve the required page order and remain readable. This guide explains what each common PDF operation actually changes, when you should keep the original, and how to validate results before sending important files.

1. When merging is the right operation

Combining files makes sense when you need one report containing a cover letter, attachments, certificates or invoices. Before merging, rename the sources into a deliberate sequence, such as 01-cover-letter and 02-attachments. Inspect the individual PDFs for blank or duplicated pages. Use the Merge PDF tool, then read the opening, closing and transition pages in the downloaded result.

Mixed files can retain different page sizes and orientations. A merged document is not automatically print-ready. Editing or recreating a digitally signed PDF may also invalidate its signature, even if the visible words have not changed. Check the recipient's requirements before merging signed documents.

2. Compression has trade-offs

Some compression methods optimize images already inside a PDF. Others render each page into a new image and wrap the images in a new PDF. The latter approach may shrink image-heavy documents but can remove searchable, selectable text or degrade fine details. PixPDFKit's compression tool uses the page-rasterization approach; that limitation is disclosed so you can make an informed choice.

Keep the unmodified original. Compare the new file size and zoom in on tables, small captions, formulas and fine lines. If the compressed output is larger or its text is no longer searchable when you need search, retain the original. For regulatory archives or contracts, preserving the original often matters more than saving a few megabytes.

3. JPG or PNG for exported pages?

JPG generally works well for photographs and graded tones at manageable file sizes, though heavier compression may introduce artifacts around text and edges. PNG preserves pixel details without additional lossy encoding, making it appealing for charts, sharp graphics and small lettering, often with larger files. Try the PDF to JPG or PDF to PNG tool using a representative page first.

Exported images do not magically retain selectable text or digital signatures. These tools do not perform optical character recognition (OCR). For a scanned PDF, changing formats cannot reveal detail that was not captured by the scanner. If a recipient requires an original PDF or a signed document, avoid substituting image pages.

4. Make a usable PDF from phone photos

Place documents on a flat surface with good, even lighting. Keep the edges visible and photograph all pages in a consistent orientation. Then use Images to PDF. Open the resulting PDF before sharing: check that text remains readable, no edge is cropped and sideways pages are corrected. Saving a low-quality photo as a PDF does not restore missing detail.

Look around the background of your photos before you send them. Personal names, bills or unrelated paperwork may be visible beyond the intended document. Only retain the pages needed for the recipient.

5. Privacy and local processing

PixPDFKit's current tools process document content in the browser; the site's application code does not upload PDFs to its own server. This differs from tools that ask users to upload every document. Browser processing still depends on the device's security. External library and font providers may observe network requests and technical data such as IP addresses, and hosting providers may keep routine access logs. Read the site's privacy policy for the current technical disclosure.

Use an up-to-date browser and a trusted device. Avoid processing sensitive contracts or personal information on a shared public computer. Clear downloaded copies when you no longer need them, and do not include confidential attachments in routine support messages.

6. A final quality-control checklist

  1. Preserve the original files under separate names before making changes.
  2. Confirm the page count and the order of appendices and sections.
  3. Open the result in another reader or on another device if possible.
  4. Zoom in on the smallest text, tables, graphics and scans.
  5. Test searching and copying text when that functionality matters.
  6. Verify signatures, links and interactive fields, which editing may alter.
  7. Check the final filename and document images for unintended private data.

7. Pick a tool for the task

Use merging to combine documents, splitting or extraction for selected pages, organizing for a new order, conversion to JPG or PNG for image sharing, and images to PDF for photographs.
